\begin{abstract}
Before the challenge, we designed a framework for allocating computation across matrix product state and Pauli-path approximations. Sample calibration and graded submissions then changed how we selected representations, tested precision, and decided whether further computation was useful. Reusing exact results supplied G1's reported 300/300. Kevin's G3 threshold refinement raised his reported score from 534/800 to 773/800. Repository reports place two mixed-precision G2 submissions at 579/600 and 580/600, although their accounts and source jobs remain unresolved. G4 received an accepted zero, and held-out tests gave no support for extrapolation or fusion. We use these outcomes to document the framework's development. Its performance against other allocation policies under equal budgets remains unmeasured.
\end{abstract}

\section{Observable and scoring objective}
Each published identifier \file{Duke_<problem>_<q>} denotes a separate program, not another measurement on a shared simulated state. For its circuit $U_{c,q}$, the target is
\begin{equation}
 F^*_{c,q}=\langle 0^{n_c}|U_{c,q}^{\dagger}Z_qU_{c,q}|0^{n_c}\rangle.
\end{equation}
The SDK's Pauli string places qubit zero at the right. Correct ID coverage, observable construction, and ordering were prerequisites to every numerical comparison. The public samples contain 4, 16, and 30 IDs; G1 to G4 contain 30, 36, 36, and 38. Only the samples expose circuit QASM.\cite{challenge}

The two approximation families compress different objects. MPS represents the state by a chain of tensors; its bond dimension $\chi$ limits the Schmidt rank retained across each cut. Increasing $\chi$ allows more entanglement to be represented but does not by itself certify the target observable. PPS propagates an observable as a sum $O=\sum_P c_P P$ of Pauli strings. Clifford conjugation maps a Pauli string to another Pauli string, whereas non-Clifford gates can branch that expansion. Removing small coefficients controls its size while changing cancellations and the final expectation. This explains our chain-versus-mostly-Clifford method prior, and why a PPS coefficient threshold is not a final-observable error bound.\cite{design,kevin}

The retained challenge rules award a problem of weight $w_c$ the score
\begin{equation}
 S_c=w_c\bigl[\max(0,\rho(\widehat{\mathbf F}_c,\mathbf F_c^*))\bigr]^2,
 \qquad
 \rho(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=
 \frac{\mathbf x_0^\top\mathbf y_0}{\|\mathbf x_0\|_2\|\mathbf y_0\|_2},
 \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf x_0=\mathbf x-\bar x\mathbf1$ and similarly for $\mathbf y_0$; constant predictions score zero. Weights are 100 for each sample and 300, 600, 800, and 1000 for G1 to G4.\cite{rules}

This objective rewards positively correlated \emph{shape}. A common offset or positive scale leaves Pearson correlation unchanged; a sign reversal does not. Ordering is informative but insufficient: Pearson correlation also depends on relative spacings. For nonconstant matched vectors, fitting $a=(\mathbf x_0^\top\mathbf y_0)/(\mathbf x_0^\top\mathbf x_0)$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\|\mathbf y_0-a\mathbf x_0\|_2^2}{\|\mathbf y_0\|_2^2}=1-\rho^2.
 \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
We therefore inspected signed correlation, affine residuals, zero counts, and order changes alongside pointwise increments. Equation~\eqref{eq:residual} describes agreement between approximations, not their error against hidden truth. Negative slopes still matter, two nonconstant points always have $|\rho|=1$, and weak centered signals can make small absolute errors consequential.

\subsection{Choosing the next computation}
Let $E_t$ contain the vectors, settings, jobs, costs, and attributed feedback available at time $t$. An experiment $a$ specifies an ID, simulator, representation, precision, and hardware. Before running it, record the decision it tests and how different outcomes would affect the next action. If the decision would be unchanged, first check whether existing results already answer the question.

We first check whether the representation produces useful signal, then examine signed changes as precision increases and compare informative results from another method. Affine residuals measure profile changes after removing a common offset and positive scale. Dollar commitments, elapsed time, quotas, and the risk of leaving an incomplete vector constrain which tests can proceed. These checks address different uncertainties: a completed expensive job may add little information, while a cheap row near a sign change may alter the next allocation.

For a matched pair of centered profiles, define $r=\mathbf y_0-a\mathbf x_0$ using the fitted slope from Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual}. The influence $I_q=r_q^2/\|r\|_2^2$, when defined, ranks where the observed shape discrepancy is concentrated. It is a way to select complementary tests, not a probability that row $q$ is wrong. Retain separated interior and sign-sensitive rows so a panel does not depend entirely on a few dominant coordinates.

The framework combines established MPS, PPS, residual analysis, and adaptive allocation methods for opaque circuits scored by correlation. Saved records connect each question to its computation and subsequent decision. This case study documents that integration and its empirical revisions; broader originality and general performance claims would require a literature comparison and prospective evaluation.

\subsection*{Sample calibration}
Public circuits permitted comparison with independently computed exact values. A stricter local calibration selected S1 MPS $\chi=8$, S2 MPS $\chi=128$, and S3 PPS $\eps=0.001$, with maximum errors approximately $2.7\times10^{-15}$, $0.00329$, and $0.00653$. Those settings differ from the actual sample uploads in Table~\ref{tab:results}; a rounded 100/100 did not certify pointwise exactness.\cite{calibration}

For G2's one-dimensional Haar chain, Kevin additionally built a 24-qubit proxy at the graded chain's depth. Its MPS squared correlation rose from about 0.997 at $\chi=64$ to 1.000 at $\chi=128$; PPS at $\eps=0.001$ reached about 0.974. For G3's mostly-Clifford grid, the structurally related S3 favored PPS over MPS. These tests supported \emph{which family to refine}. They did not forecast the graded scores, and no equally matched public family established a reliable prior for G4.\cite{kevin}

\section{Decisions behind the highest documented scores}
\subsection{G1: reuse of exact results}
G1's 30-qubit programs fitted the available exact CPU route. Kevin's complete vector had per-job evidence and a reported score of 300/300. Importing it avoided 30 duplicate simulations. The original account separately confirmed submission, but its score remains unknown.\cite{kevin,ledger}

\subsection{G2: precision and resource limits}
Both the sample chain and depth-matched proxy favored MPS for G2. Kevin's submitted $\chi=128$ vector scored a reported 498/600 with account-attributed row receipts. A partial $\chi=256$ follow-up still moved q2 from approximately 0.916 to 0.854. Quotes near \$10.88 and seven hours for an interior job, and roughly \$360 for the full run, led him to stop. Those estimates were not incurred charges, and the stop left numerical convergence unresolved.\cite{kevin,ledger}

Later analysis on the original account found that q0/q18/q35 gave $\rho\approx0.99999965$ between $\chi=16$ and 32 because saturated endpoints dominated the panel. Subsequent $\chi=64$ checks moved q9 from 0.494442 to 0.301455 and q31 from 0.551690 to 0.416197. The transition rows revealed sensitivity missed by the endpoints, motivating broader diagnostic selection. This analysis followed Kevin's 498/600 result.\cite{decisions,ledger}

Repository note \file{030518f} reports 579/600 for a complete $\chi=64$ baseline with 23 values replaced by completed GPU $\chi=512$ results. The user confirmed that CSV was submitted. Commit \file{e0ca909} subsequently adds \file{G2_23_512_and_13_128.csv} with the message ``G2 with 580/600.'' Comparing the files gives 23 unchanged values and 13 changed values, consistent with the later filename's claimed precision mix. The latter is the highest repository-reported G2 score. Neither report includes the submitting account, attempt, penalty, website receipt, or row-to-job mapping. The records also leave the choice of the 23 rows unexplained. They motivate testing selective precision, but cannot establish its efficiency or a controlled score gain.\cite{ledger}

\subsection{G3: resolve representation failure before tightening precision}
PPS performance depended on a native transpilation option, not only its truncation threshold. At $\eps=0.01$, the known sample \file{Duke_S3_1} returned approximately $-0.595$ and $-0.580$ at native levels 0 and 1, versus an exact value near $-0.538$; levels 2 and 3 returned zero. Stored-ID and uploaded-QASM tests did not remove the behavior. Native default level 2 also yielded constant-zero G3 results in the recorded tests.\cite{kevin}

We selected level 1 before tightening the threshold. Kevin suggested that lighter transpilation preserves useful Clifford structure, although the service's internal mechanism was not independently established. The measurements support this setting for the tested circuits.

\section{Unsuccessful refinements}
\subsection{G4: local contraction did not generalize}
The complete G4 $\chi=32$ artifact passed local format and provenance checks but received an accepted, user-reported 0/1000. On ten matched $\chi=16/32$ rows, $\rho=0.85912$, affine residual energy was 26.19\%, and 12 of 45 pairs reversed order. q0/q4 contributed 60.49\% of that subset's residual energy, motivating targeted checks. Residual influence located disagreement between approximations, not hidden error.\cite{g4,ledger}

All four $\chi=64$ jobs completed within their diagnostic elapsed thresholds and recorded zero charge. Their feasibility left the cost and value of a full 38-row run unresolved. A q4 check using native level-1 PPS returned zero at both $\eps=0.001$ and 0.0001, while compute time rose from 5.350 to 178.913 seconds, a factor of 33.4. With no recovered signal, these results gave little reason to tighten the threshold again.\cite{decisions,gpu}

\subsection{Partial G3 work did not produce the best documented result}
The original-account $\eps=0.0005$ G3 panel motivated a bounded continuation in an earlier review. That later cohort was stopped when the user changed direction, with 9 of 36 rows completed and two jobs canceled. The retained stop snapshot records no owned running jobs and known terminal costs; it does not represent a complete candidate or submission.\cite{stop} This work did not cause Kevin's 773/800: that finer, complete $\eps=0.0003$ vector was already available in his separate records. Reusing it supersedes the rationale for duplicating a coarser full cohort.\cite{ledger,decisions}

\subsection{Held-out tests of extrapolation and fusion}
Offline geometric/Aitken extrapolation on saved S2 precision histories worsened held-out next-level MAE in both evaluated windows: 0.04676 versus 0.04447, and 0.01344 versus 0.00955 for the direct estimate. The second window also lost about 3.86 points against exact values. Leave-one-circuit-family-out fusion showed no held-out gain. Both remained disabled.\cite{rejections,calibration}

We required a transformation to improve a held-out comparison before using it. Smooth trends and agreement between biased methods did not supply that evidence. We also kept whole-problem feedback separate from per-row corrections.

\section{Runtime and cost}
\subsection{Hardware acceleration is conditional on the workload}
A matched G4 q0 trial at $\chi=32$ produced CPU/GPU values differing by only $6.63\times10^{-12}$. GPU compute time was 24.189 seconds versus CPU's 48.593, but elapsed time fell only from 55.055 to 44.804 seconds: a 10.25-second saving for \$0.20 rather than the CPU receipt's \$0. The comparison established consistency and timing at one setting, not better mathematical precision. CPU $\chi=64$ subsequently fitted its diagnostic limits, so another same-precision GPU timing repeat was deferred.\cite{gpu}

The supplied paper motivates testing GPU routes when they make finer approximations attainable. It also cautions against universal speedup claims: in its examples, 96-qubit QFT at $\chi=64$ favored CPU, whereas a large kicked-Ising PPS workload strongly favored GPU. Its PPS error curve was nonmonotonic. These workload-specific observations do not certify hidden Duke values, and its target-bit overlap metric is not state fidelity or Pearson score.\cite{paper}

The campaign obtained dated GPU quotes, including PPS estimates of 0.1 seconds; measured runtime was still needed to assess them. No high-$\chi$ GPU or GPU-PPS score improvement was verified. Qiskit, Quantum Rings, Julia, and related local alternatives require gates or QASM, which the hidden circuits do not expose.\cite{gpu,decisions}

\subsection{Cost evidence and stopping conditions remain separate}
The operating budget was \$30, with a \$27 planning ceiling. Admission considered recorded charges and outstanding/new quoted commitments, not only settled spend. Samples recorded zero cloud charges; Kevin's submitted results report zero total credits; the original-account stop snapshot records \$0.20 in known charges, consistent with the matched GPU trial. These account-specific records are not a complete final billing statement or a promise that future CPU work is free. Quotes, elapsed cancellation thresholds, quota use, terminal status, and billed cost answer different questions.\cite{ledger,gpu,workflow,stop}

The saved workflow has status \file{PAUSED_NO_NEW_RUNS}. The following procedure incorporates the observations above; earlier experiments used earlier versions. If the user resumes execution:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item Reuse complete vectors and check receipts, account attribution, exact options, and unresolved jobs before proposing computation.
\item Separate representation tests from precision tests. Include transition, sign-sensitive, and interior rows when they can change the next decision.
\item Review signed changes, residuals, zeros, and ordering together. Independent agreement is useful only when both methods resolve signal.
\item Choose hardware after checking the quote against dollar, time, and quota commitments. Assign one execution owner and reconcile terminal jobs.
\item Record whether work stopped because of resource limits, user instruction, insufficient information, candidate selection, or evidence of convergence.
\end{enumerate}

The automated exporter currently requires a complete cohort with one device and identical options. Supporting the separately submitted G2 hybrids would require a row manifest and reproducible assembly. Their reported 579/600 and 580/600 scores justify investigating that extension, while the missing source records prevent attribution of gains to a specific allocation rule. Format checks alone cannot establish numerical readiness. An early fallback also does not reduce the time penalty attached to a later best-scoring result.\cite{ledger,workflow,rules}

\subsection{Limitations and further evaluation}
The pre-challenge design, saved experiments, and successive revisions document how the team developed the procedure. Kevin's fixed-representation G3 refinement provides the clearest within-path graded improvement. The higher G2 reports have weaker attribution, and G4 remains unresolved.

We have not compared this framework with exhaustive precision sweeps, fixed allocation, or another adaptive policy under equal budgets. A prospective evaluation would fix public circuit families and resource budgets, hold out larger instances, and compare score, elapsed time, and cost. Removing representation tests, row selection, and shape diagnostics in separate experiments would help identify which decisions improve performance. These experiments remain proposed work.

\section{Reproducibility and evidence boundaries}
All conclusions above use retained files; no simulation, quotation, submission, or authenticated account inspection was performed for this report. The result ledger is the current authority for account attribution. Older dated reviews preserve the reasoning available at the time, including proposals later superseded by terminal receipts, teammate imports, or the user-directed pause.

To reproduce the audit offline, align each CSV to its published ID manifest, verify finite direct values and the intended observable, check its SHA256 against the receipt, and join rows to the recorded device/options/job IDs. Recompute comparisons only after aligning IDs. Byte equality and numerical equality are distinct: Kevin's G2 $\chi=128$ CSV begins with hash \file{a92e6e44}, the original $\chi=32$ CSV with \file{4cb0c3c1}, and Kevin's G3 v3 with \file{44fdbe22}. Kevin's provenance records identify \file{kevin/experiment}; imported results were consolidated by commit \file{2a3eb2b}. The audit should preserve both original and teammate evidence rather than using one account's results to settle another's runtime state.
