## Feasible controls and blocked transfers

**Available without circuit extraction:** native named-ID MPS bond dimension; supported CPU/GPU MPS device choice; PPS coefficient threshold at or above 1e-5; native transpilation levels 0-3; bounded scheduling, quote checks and elapsed-time measurement. These are supplied campaign/SDK constraints, not promises made by the paper.

**Not established by the supplied API:** GPU PPS access for these named IDs, explicit retained-Pauli counts, arbitrary Pauli-weight cutoff, tensor ordering, contraction-tree optimization, gate rewrites, operator light-cone simplification, or hardware SKU selection. The paper mentioning a method or research backend does not expose its controls to this account.

**Blocked without QASM/circuit access:** moving hidden graded IDs to local Quimb, qsim, Qiskit pauli-prop, PauliPropagation.jl, Quantum Rings or cuPauliProp; choosing a custom tensor-network layout; changing gate decompositions locally; applying the peaked-circuit target-bit reconstruction attack. The paper's local-Z `pauli_sum` example does not permit merging different Duke circuit IDs into one circuit. Exact GPU requests at 36/38 qubits were already rejected, and the paper's smaller state-vector benchmark does not overturn those receipts.

## Scoring, resource and interpretation limits

The campaign seeks graded accuracy improvement, whereas the paper mostly measures runtime and a single-observable error or a target-bit overlap. Positive affine transformations preserve Pearson correlation; therefore, increasing amplitude alone cannot establish a score improvement. Comparing two approximations measures their sensitivity, not their correlation with unknown truth. Near-constant vectors and shared bias are especially hazardous. Keep raw direct estimates; do not sign-flip, affine-correct, splice pilot values, extrapolate, or fit hidden labels from scores.

Retain the $30 total budget, $27 working exposure ceiling, $0 sample spending, fresh exact-action quotes, committed quotes until terminal known cost, deadline/submission buffer, sole execution owner, and known GPU rolling/total quotas (45 minutes per hour and 7.5 hours total). Prior $0 CPU charges never waive a new quote. Cancellation requests are not termination or zero charge. This review authorizes no launch.

All paper authors are employed by or affiliated with BlueQubit (p. 13). Its local CPU PPS baselines used a 16 GB laptop; native MPS/PPS CPU used two cores; MPS GPU used A100, and PPS GPU included H100, H200 and MI300X (pp. 3-4, 12). The paper explicitly omits symmetric cost-to-solution accounting, compile/transpile timing, and wider workload coverage (p. 12). Thus its speedups are workload/hardware-specific and cannot predict our dollar savings. Some prose gives 2-3x or greater-than-3x speedup at chi256 while individual build/depth comparisons are about 2x; preserve the specific experiment instead of a universal ratio (pp. 7-9).

The chi5000 runtime numbers are extrapolations, not measured executions (pp. 6-7). The finest PPS reference value 0.2955 is adopted from converged tensor-network work, whose reported absolute error is less than .01, rather than an independently exact calculation performed in this paper (p. 11). NVIDIA cuPauliProp points are inferred from published speedup ratios, not directly measured here, with unspecified accelerator in the comparison (pp. 10-11). These limits strengthen the case for matched local pilots and independent numerical review.
