## The paper motivates precision-dependent routing, not accuracy guarantees

In the supplied *Benchmarking Zero-Setup Quantum Circuit Simulators*, the 40-qubit quantum-volume MPS experiment fits runtime exponents of 2.03 for CPU and 1.49 for GPU over its tested range. A different peaked circuit crosses toward GPU around chi128, while 96-qubit QFT at chi64 takes roughly 90 seconds on GPU versus 12 on CPU. The same implementation can therefore favor different hardware as precision and circuit structure change. The chi5000 figures are extrapolations, and the peaked chi1536 run takes about 22 hours; neither is a practical forecast for this campaign. ([Supplied paper, pp. 6–9, Figs. 3–4, 7 and 9](<D:/BluQubit/papers/alternative approaches.pdf>))

The paper's PPS GPU result is striking but narrow: on one 127-qubit kicked-Ising observable, threshold 0.000025 retains 27.6 million Paulis and takes about 3.17–3.52 seconds on its GPUs versus 5714 seconds on its native CPU. Its error curve is **nonmonotonic**: approximately 0.015 at 0.005, 0.14 near 0.00005, and 0.016 at 0.00000289. That last value uses a tensor-network reference with its own reported error below 0.01, rather than an independently exact statevector. The example supports comparing coarse and fine settings; it does not establish the best threshold for an OTOC vector, and the lowest illustrated threshold is below our SDK's supported floor. ([Supplied paper, pp. 9–11, Figs. 10–12](<D:/BluQubit/papers/alternative approaches.pdf>))

The paper's 100% peaked-circuit overlap measures recovery of target bits from signs of local-Z expectations. It is not 100% state fidelity, exact observable values, or a perfect Pearson score. Its authors disclose BlueQubit affiliation, asymmetric hardware and omitted full cost-to-solution and compilation timing. Those limitations call for matched measurements with our IDs, options, elapsed time and actual billing. Our earlier G4 q0 chi32 CPU/GPU pair agreed within 6.63e-12, but GPU saved only **10.25 seconds elapsed** and billed **$0.20**, compared with $0 on that CPU receipt. This is implementation-consistency evidence at one setting; two matching approximations can share the same bias. ([Supplied paper, pp. 3–5, 9 and 12–13](<D:/BluQubit/papers/alternative approaches.pdf>), [matched local trial](gpu_matched_pilot.json))

The scoring rule rewards positive Pearson correlation squared; common offsets and positive scaling can leave it unchanged while individual values remain inaccurate. Conversely, small absolute errors can reverse the shape of a weak true signal. The fidelity-to-observable connection also needs care: for normalized pure states with squared overlap fidelity F and a Pauli observable Z, the trace-norm bound gives **|delta expectation(Z)| <= 2 sqrt(1 - F)**. Thus F = 0.9999 only guarantees an absolute observable error no larger than 0.02 by this worst-case bound. This is a mathematical bound, not a measured error; no such fidelity certificate is available for the hidden IDs, and the paper's target-bit overlap cannot substitute for F. Full-vector shape diagnostics therefore belong beside pointwise sensitivity and independent-method comparisons, while retaining the distinction between agreement and unknown truth. ([Paper fidelity definition, p. 5](<D:/BluQubit/papers/alternative approaches.pdf>), [retained scoring rules](../docs/history/workflow_v1.md:70))

An approximate export explicitly records `submission_recommended: false` and `NUMERICAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED`; the compatibility field `submission_eligible` covers format and resolved signal only. Every proposed upload still needs a complete, single-device, identical-options vector with direct job provenance and a documented numerical review. No pilot splicing, sign reversal, row permutation, affine correction, extrapolation, fusion or fitting to scores is enabled. ([Export gate](../src/otoc/graded.py:370), [workflow](../workflow_final.md))

Attempts should be conserved by doing diagnostics through simulator evidence. **An early weak submission is fallback insurance; it does not reduce the time penalty of a later best score**, whose timestamp contributes to ranking. Fresh exact-action quotes, settled costs, outstanding commitments, genuine missing-baseline reserves, total/rolling GPU quotas, the sole runner lock and the submission buffer continue to govern each launch. The existing failed G4 artifact can satisfy a mechanical coverage exemption without becoming a scientifically trusted baseline. Cancellation requests do not settle cost or authorize duplicate work. ## Conclusion

The practical opportunity is to spend on evidence that changes a decision: whether a finer representation is tractable, whether its profile remains unstable, and whether hardware helps reach that representation in time. The two completed CPU chi64 trials show that conservative quotes and observed runtime must remain distinct, while the changed values show why faster execution alone cannot settle quality. GPU access is evidenced at the quote level; expansion depends on a bounded measured result and numerical review, not on transferring a benchmark's speedup or another account's score.

A recommendation can improve before any new official attempt is used. Preserve the current artifacts, complete the next informative comparison, and commit a coherent refinement only when its evidence and remaining resources justify it. This supports maximum graded accuracy with fewer submissions while keeping the remaining uncertainty explicit.
